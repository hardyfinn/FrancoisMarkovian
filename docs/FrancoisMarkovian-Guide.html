FRANCOIS MARKOVIAN

Three VCV Rack modules for patterns that jump, answer each other and steer themselves.

Overview

The plugin holds three modules that work on their own and get more interesting together.

Francois Markovian 29 HP

A trigger sequencer whose playhead jumps between 8 steps by chances you draw in a matrix. Six tracks, A to F, each with its own chance per step. Two snapshots (X and Y) and an Evolve control morph between them, Freeze locks what just played, and a clock divider output keeps other gear on the grid.

Interlock 16 HP

A trigger processor where tracks answer each other: when one fires, it raises or lowers the others' chances on the next steps. It can hold hits back or add ghost hits.

Governor 18 HP

A feedback controller that listens to the triggers, measures how busy (density) and how changeable (variety) they are, and outputs control CVs that push the music back towards targets you set.

Install

  1. Copy FrancoisMarkovian-2.3.0-mac-arm64.vcvplugin into your Rack 2 plugins folder (Rack: Help > Open user folder, then plugins-mac-arm64). Replace any older version.
  2. Restart Rack. The three modules appear under the brand Haer.

Quick start

  1. Add Francois Markovian. Patch a 16th-note clock into CLOCK (from Live via the disting NT and CV Tools, or VCV's Clocked).
  2. Patch outputs A and B to two drum voices. You'll hear A on step 1 and B on step 5.
  3. In the matrix, drag the cell at row 4, column 1 up to about 30. Now step 4 sometimes jumps back to step 1.
  4. Drag some trigger cells on track C up to 40–70% and listen to it come and go.
  5. Click EDIT Y, draw a busier pattern, and turn EVOLVE slowly from X to Y.
Tip. A new module plays straight (1 to 8 in order), so it behaves like a normal sequencer until you add jumps. Add one jump at a time and listen.

Francois Markovian

The jump matrix (left)

Each row is the step the playhead is on (FROM), and each column is where it can go next (TO). Every cell shows its chance as a number and a bar, and each row always adds up to 100.

  • Drag a cell up or down to set its chance. The rest of the row rescales and keeps its own balance: with 30 / 70 in a row, dragging a third cell to 50 leaves 15 / 35 / 50. Shift drags finely.
  • Double-click switches a cell between 0 and 100.
  • The diagonal (a step going to itself) gives stutters and rolls.
  • A row with nothing in it falls back to the next step, shown as a small outline.
  • The lighter row is where the playhead is now. A white outline marks the jump it just took.

Trigger chances (right)

Each of the 6 × 8 cells is the chance that the track fires when the playhead lands on that step. Drag or double-click it like the matrix. The playhead's column is lighter, and a cell flashes when it fires.

Snapshots X and Y, and Evolve

There are two complete snapshots, each with its own matrix and trigger chances. EDIT X / Y chooses which one the grids show and edit, and a white line in a cell marks the other snapshot's value. EVOLVE blends every cell from X to Y. A trigger at 0 in X and 100 in Y plays half the time at the halfway point. The tick under the X / Y switch shows where Evolve is.

Freeze

FREEZE (button toggle, or the GATE input while high) loops the last 8 steps exactly as they were played, jumps, triggers and dice included. Let go and the playhead carries on under the matrix again. Reset while frozen restarts the loop.

Clock divider

DIV (1, 2, 4, 8, 16) and OFFSET (0, 1, 2, 4 pulses) set the DIV output: a trigger on every Nth input clock, counted from Reset and shifted later by the offset. /4 +2 fires on clocks 3, 7, 11 and so on. It follows the input clock, not the jumping playhead.

ControlWhat it does
CLOCKEach pulse moves the playhead once.
RESETThe next clock plays step 1 and restarts the divider.
EVOLVE + CVMorph X to Y. 0 to 10 V adds 0 to 100%.

Right-click menu: Straight (reset the matrix of X, Y or both) · Randomise (Gentle keeps "next step" strongest and adds one or two jumps, Wild puts chances anywhere) · Copy X to Y / Y to X · Clear triggers.

Interlock

The 6 × 6 grid says: when track X fires, track Y's chance on the next step changes by this much (−100 to +100). The row is the track that fired, and the column is the track that gets pushed.

On every step, each track starts at 100% if a trigger came in and 0% if not. The influences of recently fired tracks are added on top, kept between 0 and 100. So a red cell can hold back an incoming hit, and a green cell can add a ghost hit where nothing came in.

  • Drag up (green, push) or down (red, hold back). Double-click clears a cell.
  • The NOW meters show how much each track is being pushed on the current step.
  • Influences add up: two tracks each pushing E by +50 give E a full 100%.

Worked example

Right-click and choose Load the guide's example to get this grid:

ControlWhat it does
CLOCKEach pulse starts a step. Unpatched, the first incoming trigger of a step starts it. Use the same clock as the sequencer.
RESETClears the memory.
AMOUNT + CVScales the whole grid. 0 = straight through, 100% = as drawn.
MEMORY0 = an influence lasts only the next step. 1 to 4 = that many extra steps, fading out (memory 2: 100%, 67%, 33%).
IN / OUT A to FTriggers in, 5 ms triggers out.
Timing. Incoming triggers are passed on (or held back) the moment they arrive, so there's no delay. Ghost hits come 2 ms after the clock, which gives triggers from another module time to arrive first.

Governor

Governor is a feedback loop: it listens, compares with a target and pushes, over and over. Patch its control CVs into whatever shapes the pattern, and the system steers itself.

What it measures

Over the last WINDOW clocks (8, 16, 32 or 64, default 16):

  • Density is the share of possible hits that played.
  • Variety is how different this window is from the one before it: 0% is an exact repeat and 100% is nothing in common. It starts measuring after two full windows.

Only connected trigger inputs count.

Reading the graphs

One point per clock. The coloured line is the measurement, the white line the target, and the yellow line the control it's sending.

Each loop (density row and variety row)

ControlWhat it does
TARGET + CVThe level to hold. 0 to 10 V adds 0 to 100%.

ControlWhat it does
CLOCK / RESETOne pulse per step, the same clock as the sequencer. Reset clears the history.

WOBBLE0 settles smoothly. Towards full it pushes harder with less damping, so it overshoots and hunts around the target.
Getting it to steer. Governor can only hold a target if its control actually changes the thing it measures. If a line drifts away from the target and the control pins at 0 or 10 V, flip POL, or make sure the patched control has room to move (for example, make sure Y really is busier than X). See Governor recipes for step-by-step setups.

The full system

All three together: the sequencer jumps, Interlock makes the parts answer each other, and Governor listens to the result and steers both.

  1. One clock (16ths) goes to all three modules' CLOCK inputs, and one reset goes to all three RESET inputs.
  2. Markovian A–F to Interlock IN A–F, and Interlock OUT A–F to your drum voices.
  3. Interlock OUT A–F also go to Governor's A–F inputs (use a multiple, or stacked cables).
  4. Governor density CTRL to Markovian EVOLVE CV, with EVOLVE knob at 0, and Y as the busier snapshot.
  5. Governor variety CTRL to Interlock AMOUNT CV, with AMOUNT knob at 0.

Governor recipes

Governor can only steer what its control CVs are patched into. These are starting points for the modules in your rack.

Governor → Francois Markovian

Governor slides the pattern between snapshot X and Y to reach the density you want.

  1. Clock and reset: send the same clock to both modules' CLOCK inputs and the same reset to both RESET inputs.
  2. Let Governor listen: patch Markovian's A–F outs to your drums and to Governor's A–F ins. In Rack you can stack a second cable on an output by Ctrl-dragging (Cmd on Mac) from it.
  3. Make X sparse and Y busy: right-click Markovian, Copy X to Y, then switch EDIT to Y and drag more trigger chances up.
  4. Patch the control: Governor density CTRL → Markovian EVOLVE CV. Turn Markovian's EVOLVE knob to 0 (the CV adds to the knob). Density POL down (+).
  5. Set a target: density TARGET about 40%, RESPONSE at noon, WOBBLE at 0. The coloured line should move onto the white target line within a few bars.

Bonus: variety VARIED gate → Markovian FREEZE gate. It locks into a loop when things get too chaotic, and lets go once the loop gets too repetitive.

Governor → Seuperclidean

The 6-channel Seuperclidean has DENSITY CV and MUTATE CV inputs, so each Governor loop gets an obvious target.

  1. Send the same 16th clock and reset to both modules.
  2. Stack-patch Seuperclidean's GATE outs (rows 1–6) to your drums and to Governor's A–F ins.
  3. Density: Governor density CTRL → Seuperclidean DENSITY CV, POL down (+). Turn Seuperclidean's DENSITY knob fully left. Its CV input is ±5 V added to the knob, so from fully left Governor's 0–10 V sweeps the whole range, from fewest hits to most. From noon, anything above 5 V does nothing and Governor can only add hits.
  4. Variety: Governor variety CTRL → Seuperclidean MUTATE CV (0–10 V = full range), MUTATE knob at 0, POL down (+). Give each row some MUT (about 30–50%) so there is something to scale. Governor raises mutation when the groove has been static and lowers it when it's changing too much, and Seuperclidean's homing keeps it from drifting off for good.

Alternative for variety: variety VARIED gate → Seuperclidean LOCK gate. When things get too changeable, the current mutated patterns freeze until variety drops back.

Seuperclidean 4 has no density or mutate CV inputs. Multiple the density CTRL into each voice's FILL CV instead (10 V adds a full length of hits), with the FILL knobs set low.

Starting points: density target 35–45%, variety target 20–30%, RESPONSE at noon, WOBBLE at 0.

Patch ideas

Drunk drummer

Straight matrix, then a 20–30% "back to step 1" in rows 4 and 8, and a little diagonal on the hat's steps. Recognisable, but never quite the same bar twice.

Breath in, breath out

X sparse, Y dense. A slow LFO (or Live automation) into EVOLVE CV gives a pattern that fills and empties over 16–32 bars.

Call and response

In Interlock, set A to B at −100 and B to C at +60, with Memory 1. The kick shuts the snare up, and the snare sets off percussion just after.

Self-locking loop

Governor variety VARIED gate to Markovian FREEZE gate. When things get too chaotic it locks into the last bar. Once the loop gets too repetitive, the gate drops and it lets go again.

Hunting groove

Governor density with WOBBLE high and RESPONSE medium. The pattern swells and thins around the target on its own, like a band that can't quite settle.

Polyrhythm from the divider

DIV at /4 with offset +2 into a hat or a filter envelope: an off-beat pulse that stays on the grid while the main pattern jumps around.

Arrangement with Live

Here is how these modules help with loop fatigue: instead of copying clips, you automate a handful of targets.

  • Send Live's clock and reset to the rig through the disting NT (CV Tools), and use the same pulses in VCV.
  • Automate Governor density TARGET per section (intro 20%, groove 45%, peak 75%, breakdown 15%). The system finds its own way to each level, so every pass sounds different.
  • Automate Governor variety TARGET low for sections that should lock in, and high for transitions.
  • Hit FREEZE (via a Live MIDI-to-gate) for drops and hooks, then let go to release it.
  • Record long takes and arrange afterwards: cut the best 8–16-bar moments into scenes.
